    Theme and data source

    Instrument and report details

    Printed in the PDF report (ISO 18738 6.5 a).

    Sensor check (phone as instrument)

    Put the phone flat and still, then run a 10-second check. It tests sampling rate, gravity (0 Hz accuracy), noise, resolution and the vibration noise floor, as in ISO 18738 4.6.3. The date is saved as the “last check” in the report.

    Limits for pass / fail

    ISO 18738 gives the measuring method only, not acceptable values. Reference = default set (speed 92–105 % of rated and acceleration 1.5 m/s² as in GB/T 10058, the rest common screening values). Manual = your own values, e.g. from the contract.

    Sound level (microphone)

    The phone / PC microphone gives an indicative A-weighted level (FAST, 125 ms). For trustworthy values calibrate once against a sound level meter: put both side by side in a steady noise (e.g. a fan), type the meter reading and tap Calibrate.

    Analysis settings

    Defaults follow the standard (10 Hz 2-pole Butterworth, τ = 2 s). A lower low-pass (e.g. 2–4 Hz) gives smoother acceleration and jerk on a noisy phone, but is no longer the ISO value.

    User guide

    1. How to measure
    1. Keep the screen on. The app keeps it awake while recording.
    2. Place the phone flat on the car floor within about 100 mm of the centre (ISO 18738 6.2). Do not hold it, and do not put it on soft material.
    3. Tap Start and stay still for 3 seconds. The app measures gravity, the vertical and horizontal axes and the sensor noise floor.
    4. Run the lift from terminal to terminal. Wait about 1 second after the doors open, then tap Stop. The last 0.5 s is ignored to remove the touch of the Stop button.
    5. Record at least one UP and one DOWN run. At most two persons in the car, each still and quiet, feet more than 150 mm from the phone.
    2. No barometer? No problem

    Most phones have no barometer (for example the Realme 8). ISO 18738 itself obtains velocity by integrating the 10 Hz filtered vertical acceleration, so the accelerometer is enough. The app assumes the car is at rest before and after the ride and removes the sensor offset drift (zero-velocity correction). In simulated tests the height error was typically under 0.3 m and the speed error under 2 %.

    For better height, enter the known travel height; the speed and height are then scaled to it. If the offset drift is large, the app warns you and estimates the possible height error. A barometer, when present, is fused automatically.

    3. How to read the results
    • Max / A95 acceleration and deceleration: from the 10 Hz filtered vertical signal. A95 is the value that 95 % of the samples between 5 % and 95 % of maximum speed do not exceed.
    • Jerk: slope of a 0.5 s running least-squares line of the acceleration; the maximum is reported.
    • Max / V95 speed: V95 is evaluated from 1 s after the car reaches 95 % of its top speed until 1 s before it drops below 95 %.
    • Vibration (p-p): weighted signal; each peak-to-peak value is taken between zero crossings 1–3, 2–4, 3–5… Max is the largest, A95 is the value 95 % of them do not exceed. The 0-pk column is the largest single peak.
    • Constant / non-constant region: the vertical signal is split by the slope of the acceleration (above 0.3 m/s³, plus 0.5 s either side = non-constant). Door-close and start/stop transients fall in the non-constant region.
    • Boundaries: boundary 1 and 2 are 500 mm after the start and before the stop (from the integrated height). X and Y vibration are evaluated between them.
    • Charts: tap or click a curve to read time, value, the lift position in the shaft and the speed. Drag across (mouse), pinch (touch) or Ctrl + wheel to zoom; double-click or ⟲ to reset. ISO-weighted / Unfiltered switches the vibration signal, like the ISO / Raw filter of a PMT EVA. Limit lines: ±(max p-p ÷ 2) on vibration, ± limits on acceleration and jerk, 92–105 % on speed, the dB(A) limit on sound.
    • Vibration source finder: looks at the axis, the ride phase, the position and the frequency of the vibration and matches them with the lift data (sheave, roping, gear, motor poles, rollers, rail length, bracket spacing). With several runs (History → Compare) it also finds heights that repeat in up and down runs and frequencies that follow the speed.
    4. Limitations (please read)
    • A phone is not a calibrated instrument. Use this for screening, comparison and troubleshooting, not for certificates or contract disputes. For traceable ISO 18738 results use a calibrated analyser.
    • Sound level is measured with the device microphone. It is not a class 1/2 sound level meter: calibrate it (More → Sound level) and treat the values as indicative.
    • The vibration source finder lists probable causes with their evidence. Always confirm on site before replacing parts.
    • Sensor noise limits small vibrations. Run the sensor check to see your phone's noise floor; values below about twice the noise floor are only indications.
    • Phone sensors sample at up to 200 Hz, so vibration is evaluated up to about 80 Hz (40 Hz at 100 Hz sampling).
    • ISO 18738:2003 is the edition this version follows. Later editions and ISO 8100-34 may change details.
    5. Reports and data

    The PDF report follows the layout of ISO 18738 6.5 (general information, ride-quality results, performance characteristics) with charts. The CSV file has the full time series including the weighted signals and the region flags. On Android a share sheet opens after the file is made; save it to Drive, Files or send it by WhatsApp.
